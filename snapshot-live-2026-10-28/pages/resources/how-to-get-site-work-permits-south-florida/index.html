Understanding Site Work Permits in South Florida

Obtaining the necessary permits is one of the most critical and often challenging aspects of site work in South Florida. The region has some of the strictest permitting requirements in the country, with multiple agencies involved in the approval process.

This comprehensive guide walks you through the permit process for site work in Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, and Monroe Counties. Understanding what permits you need, how to apply, and what to expect helps ensure your project proceeds without delays.

Working without proper permits can result in stop-work orders, fines, and project delays. Taking time to understand and obtain all required permits upfront saves time and money in the long run.

Overview of Site Work Permits Required

Most site work projects in South Florida require multiple permits from different agencies. Here's an overview of the main permit categories:

  • Building Department Permits: Required for most site work activities including grading, excavation, and drainage
  • Environmental Permits: Required for projects affecting water resources, wetlands, or protected species
  • Tree Removal Permits: Required before removing trees or vegetation
  • Utility Permits: Required for work affecting or installing utilities
  • Right-of-Way Permits: Required for work in public rights-of-way
  • Flood Zone Permits: Special requirements for properties in flood zones

The specific permits required depend on your project type, location, and scope of work. Each county has unique requirements, so it's essential to check with local agencies.

Miami-Dade County Site Work Permits

Miami-Dade County has some of the most complex permit requirements in South Florida. Understanding these requirements is essential for successful projects.

Miami-Dade Building Department Permits

The Miami-Dade Building Department requires permits for:
  • Site grading and earthwork
  • Excavation for foundations
  • Drainage system installation
  • Utility installation
  • Erosion control measures
Permit applications must include site plans, engineering drawings, and calculations. Processing typically takes 30-60 days. Learn about Miami site work services and local requirements.

Miami-Dade DERM Permits

The Department of Environmental Resources Management (DERM) requires permits for:

  • Projects affecting wetlands or water resources
  • Tree removal and vegetation clearing
  • Projects in environmentally sensitive areas
  • Stormwater management systems

DERM permits can take 60-120 days to process. Early application is critical to avoid project delays. DERM also requires environmental surveys for many projects.

Miami-Dade DTPW Requirements

The Department of Transportation and Public Works (DTPW) regulates:

  • Work in public rights-of-way
  • Road and driveway connections
  • Utility installations in public areas
  • Traffic control during construction

DTPW permits are required before beginning work that affects public infrastructure.

Broward County Site Work Permits

Broward County has its own permit requirements that must be understood before beginning site work:

Broward Building Division Permits

The Broward County Building Division requires permits for site work including:
  • Grading and earthwork
  • Excavation operations
  • Drainage system installation
  • Utility work
Permit processing typically takes 20-45 days. Applications must include proper site plans and engineering documentation. See Fort Lauderdale site work requirements.

Broward DER Environmental Permits

Broward County's Department of Environmental Resources (DER) requires permits for:

  • Projects affecting natural resources
  • Tree removal and vegetation clearing
  • Wetland impacts
  • Stormwater management systems

DER permits must comply with the Natural Resource Protection Code. Processing can take 45-90 days depending on project complexity.

SFWMD Requirements in Broward

The South Florida Water Management District (SFWMD) requires Environmental Resource Permits for projects affecting water resources. These permits are separate from county permits and can take 60-120 days to process.

SFWMD permits are required for most site work projects that affect stormwater runoff, water quality, or water quantity.

Palm Beach County Site Work Permits

Palm Beach County has specific permit requirements that must be followed:

Palm Beach Building Division Permits

The Palm Beach County Building Division requires permits for site work activities. Applications must comply with the Unified Land Development Code (ULDC).
Permit processing typically takes 30-60 days. Required documents include site plans, engineering drawings, and stormwater calculations. Learn about West Palm Beach site work requirements.

Palm Beach ERM Review

Environmental Resources Management (ERM) review is required for many site work projects in Palm Beach County. ERM reviews:

  • Environmental impacts
  • Wetland and water resource effects
  • Protected species considerations
  • Tree removal and vegetation impacts

ERM review can add 30-60 days to the permit process. Early coordination with ERM helps identify requirements and avoid delays.

Monroe County (Florida Keys) Site Work Permits

Monroe County has the strictest environmental permit requirements in Florida due to the sensitive ecosystem of the Florida Keys:

Monroe County Building Department Permits

The Monroe County Building Department requires permits for all site work. Applications must demonstrate compliance with:

  • Flood zone elevation requirements
  • Environmental protection standards
  • Protected species compliance
  • Mangrove and seagrass protection
Permit processing can take 60-120 days due to extensive environmental review. See Florida Keys site work requirements.

Monroe County Environmental Requirements

Monroe County has the strictest environmental requirements in the state:

  • Protected species surveys: Required for most projects, can cost $2,000 to $10,000+
  • Mangrove protection: Strict regulations on mangrove removal and impacts
  • Coral reef protection: Projects must not affect nearby coral reefs
  • Seagrass protection: Strict limits on impacts to seagrass beds
  • Fill restrictions: Limited fill allowed, especially in coastal areas

Environmental compliance in the Florida Keys requires working with specialists familiar with Keys regulations and protected species requirements.

South Florida Water Management District Permits

The South Florida Water Management District (SFWMD) requires Environmental Resource Permits (ERPs) for most site work projects that affect water resources.

SFWMD permits are required for:

  • Projects affecting stormwater runoff
  • Drainage system installation
  • Retention and detention pond construction
  • Projects in wetlands or water bodies
  • Projects affecting water quality or quantity

ERP applications must include:

  • Site plans showing existing and proposed conditions
  • Stormwater calculations
  • Drainage system design
  • Environmental impact assessment
  • Erosion control plans
ERP processing typically takes 60-120 days. Expedited review may be available for smaller projects. Learn more about drainage and stormwater solutions that comply with SFWMD requirements.

Tree Removal and Vegetation Permits

Tree removal permits are required in all South Florida counties before removing trees or significant vegetation:

Tree Removal Permit Requirements

Tree removal permits typically require:

  • Tree survey identifying all trees on the property
  • Justification for tree removal
  • Replacement plan (if required)
  • Protected species assessment
  • Application fees ($100 to $500 per permit)
Protected trees (native species, large trees, or trees in specific locations) may require additional justification and replacement. Working with contractors experienced in vegetation and tree clearing helps navigate these requirements.

County-Specific Tree Ordinances

Each county has unique tree protection ordinances:

  • Miami-Dade: Strict tree protection, especially for native species
  • Broward: Tree preservation requirements with replacement ratios
  • Palm Beach: ULDC tree protection standards
  • Monroe: Strictest requirements, especially for mangroves and native trees

Understanding county-specific requirements prevents permit delays and violations.

How to Apply for Site Work Permits

The permit application process varies by county and agency, but generally follows these steps:

Pre-Application Phase

Before submitting applications, gather all required documents:

  • Site plans and engineering drawings
  • Property surveys
  • Environmental assessments (if required)
  • Tree surveys (if removing vegetation)
  • Stormwater calculations
  • Erosion control plans
  • Utility coordination letters

Pre-application meetings with county agencies can help identify all requirements and avoid application rejections.

Application Submission

Submit complete applications to the appropriate agencies:

  • Building Department for site work permits
  • Environmental Department for environmental permits
  • SFWMD for Environmental Resource Permits
  • Other agencies as required

Incomplete applications are typically rejected, causing delays. Ensure all required documents and fees are included.

Review and Approval Process

Permit review typically involves:

  • Initial review: 5-10 days to check for completeness
  • Technical review: 20-60 days for plan review and calculations
  • Environmental review: 30-90 days for environmental permits
  • Revisions: Time to address comments and resubmit
  • Final approval: Issuance of permits

The total process can take 60-180 days depending on project complexity and agency workload. Starting early is essential.

Common Permit Mistakes to Avoid

Avoiding these common mistakes saves time and prevents project delays:

  • Starting work without permits: Results in stop-work orders and fines
  • Incomplete applications: Causes rejection and delays
  • Missing environmental permits: SFWMD and county environmental permits are often overlooked
  • Not checking flood zones: Flood zone requirements significantly affect permits
  • Underestimating processing time: Allow adequate time for permit approval
  • Not coordinating with utilities: Utility permits may be required separately
  • Ignoring protected species: Florida Keys and coastal areas have strict requirements

Working with experienced contractors who understand permit requirements helps avoid these mistakes. Our network of licensed contractors is familiar with South Florida permit processes.

Permit Costs and Fees

Permit fees vary by county and project scope. Typical costs include:

  • Building Department permits: $500 to $5,000+ depending on project value
  • Environmental permits: $1,000 to $10,000+ for DERM, DER, or ERM review
  • SFWMD ERP: $1,500 to $15,000+ depending on project scope
  • Tree removal permits: $100 to $500 per permit
  • Engineering fees: $5,000 to $25,000+ for required plans and calculations
  • Surveying fees: $2,000 to $10,000+ for site surveys
  • Inspection fees: $500 to $3,000 for required inspections

Total permit costs for a typical site work project range from $5,000 to $25,000+, with complex projects exceeding $50,000 in permit fees and associated costs.

Working with Contractors on Permits

Many contractors can help with the permit process, but responsibilities vary:

  • Permit coordination: Some contractors handle permit applications
  • Plan preparation: Contractors may coordinate with engineers for required plans
  • Permit expediting: Some contractors offer expediting services
  • Owner responsibility: Ultimately, property owners are responsible for permits

Our network of licensed contractors understands South Florida permit requirements and can help coordinate the permit process. However, property owners should understand their permit responsibilities.
